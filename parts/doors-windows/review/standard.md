# The doors and windows standard

This is the standard that the reviewer uses to score a building. It says what a good set of doors and windows does. It does not tell the agent what to place: there is no hidden door count, door type or swing direction.

**Principle.** A person can enter every room that they use through a real door from the corridor or lobby. Every room where people live or work gets daylight through a window in an outside wall. Every door and window is the right type for its place, sits fully in its wall, has the right size for its use, and opens the way people need it to.

## How the reviewer scores

The reviewer first works out what **this** building needs, then looks at what the agent made. A rule that does not apply to this building is not a finding.

| Level | Meaning | Effect on the score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blocking** | The building cannot work as it is. | The score is **5 or less**. |
| **Major** | Real rework is necessary before an architect would accept it. | Several major findings give a score of about **5 to 6**. |
| **Quality** | The difference between good work and excellent work. | Good work scores **6 to 7**. Excellent work scores **8 to 10**. |

When the reviewer cannot confirm something, it writes **not assessed**. A "not assessed" item is not a pass and not a fault.

## 1. Rooms without a door (Blocking)

Every room that a person uses has a door or an open boundary to the circulation. A person does not go through a private room to get to a different private room, for example through one flat's bedroom to get to another flat's bedroom, or through a toilet to get to an office.

- One or two rooms without a way in: **Major**.
- A full floor or a full group of rooms without a way in: **Blocking**.
- Shafts, risers and the reserved stair and lift zones of the starting model do not need doors.

## 2. Rooms without a window (Major)

Bedrooms, living rooms, offices, classrooms, wards and similar rooms on an outside wall have at least one window in that wall. A window into a corridor or into another room does not give daylight to a room.

Some rooms can have no windows: stores, plant rooms, imaging rooms, auditoriums and inside toilets.

## 3. Openings that do not fit their wall (Major)

- Each door and window sits in a wall, fully inside the length and height of that wall.
- It does not overlap a different opening or a wall junction.
- It does not open into a void, a stair or a lift shaft.
- The bottom of a door is at the floor level.
- The sill height suits the room:

| Room | Window sill height |
| --- | --- |
| Bedroom or office | about 0.9 to 1.0 m |
| Above a kitchen worktop | 1.1 to 1.2 m |
| Toilet | 1.5 m or more |

- The heads of the windows on one floor are at one height.

## 4. The wrong type of door or window (Quality; Major where the list says so)

The type suits the use of the place:

- **Lift:** a sliding door, or an opening with no door, at each lift landing. Other lift doors are **Major** (see section 5).
- **Main entrance:** a double door or a wide door.
- **Escape stair:** a fire-rated door. A stair door passes when its fire rating is set. The name of the door type alone does not prove this.
- **Toilet:** a solid door, and a small top-hung or obscured window, not a full-height window.
- **Lobby, entrance hall, shop front or showroom:** large glazing or a shop front.
- **Plant room, store or core:** no window, or a small high window.
- **Room where people live or work:** a window large enough to light the room, not one small window in a large room.

## 5. Doors and windows that open the wrong way, or are too narrow

The agent's own automatic checks use the same rules and the same values.

- **A room door opens into its room**, not into a corridor, lobby, hall, gallery, concourse or atrium. A normal toilet door opens into the toilet. **Quality**; **Major** when this is true for most room doors on a floor.
  These doors can open outward: accessible toilets, showers and changing cubicles, stair and exit doors on an escape route, lifts, shafts, plant rooms, cupboards, and large rooms where many people gather, such as an auditorium, cinema, gym, sports hall, chapel, theatre or a named hall.
- **The hinge is on the side near a wall**, so that the open door rests against that wall and does not block the room. **Quality**.
- **An opening window in an outside wall opens outward**, unless the agent gives a reason for an inward window. A toilet window always opens outward. **Quality**.
- **Doors are wide enough for their use.** The clear width is the width of the gap when the door is open.

| Door | Minimum clear width | Minimum door width |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main entrance | 1.2 m | 1.2 m (a pair of doors passes) |
| Exit or stair door | 0.9 m | 1.0 m |
| Accessible toilet | 0.85 m | 0.9 m |
| Room door | 0.8 m | 0.9 m |
| Store, pantry or toilet | 0.6 m | 0.7 m |
| Cupboard, closet or shaft | any | any |

  A door that is too narrow at an entrance, an exit or an accessible toilet is **Major**. Other narrow doors are **Quality**.
- **A lift door slides**, is at least **1.2 m** wide, and opens onto a lift lobby, not onto a corridor, a stair or a room. **Major**.

## 6. Windows that do not follow a facade system (Quality; Major where the text says so)

On each elevation, the windows follow one system:

- On each floor, the window heads are at one height, and the sills are at one or two heights. A toilet's high sill and full-height glazing are permitted differences.
- The windows of a floor are above the windows of the floor below.

The reviewer also looks at the elevations as a whole: how the openings read together, solid wall against glass, and where the pattern changes and why. A ground-floor shop front, lobby or entrance can change the pattern on purpose. An upper floor of repeated rooms must not.

- Each elevation or floor that does not follow the system: **Quality**.
- Most floors of an elevation fail both tests, so the elevation is a random mix of openings: **Major**.

These items are not part of this standard: the wall types (they are fixed in the starting model), windows at stair landings (the stairs are reserved zones), and orientation, shade and glare (the request gives no site or climate).

## 7. Doors and windows that hit something when they open (Quality)

A door or a window that, when it opens, hits a wall, another door, a stair or a fixture. When the reviewer cannot confirm this, it writes "not assessed".

## 8. What the agent says about its doors and windows (Major)

What the agent says it did (the number of doors and windows, their widths, which way they open, "all rooms have windows") agrees with the model. A claim that is wrong is a claim that the agent did not check. A claim that is more than 5% wrong limits the score to 3.
